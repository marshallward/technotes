================
QR Decomposition
================

Basic Understanding
===================

What is QR decomposition?
-------------------------

Divide a matrix into an *orthonormal matrix* :math:`Q` and an upper triangular
matrix :math:`R`.


...and what is an orthonormal matrix?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

:math:`Q` is *orthonormal* if it preserves dot products after vector
transformation.  That is,

.. math::

   Q \mathbf{u} \cdot Q \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}

Write the dot product as a transpose, and

.. math::

   \mathbf{u}^T Q^T Q \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{v}

That is,

.. math::

   \mathbf{u}^T \left(Q^T Q - I\right) \mathbf{v} = 0

Or, in its more common form, :math:`Q^T Q = I`.

The most common example of an orthogonal matrix is the rotation matrix,

.. math::

   R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix}
      \cos \theta & \sin \theta \\
      -\sin \theta & \cos \theta
   \end{pmatrix}

The most interesting property of the orthogonal matrix is the *orthogonality*
of its column vectors.  If :math:`Q = ( \mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2,
\mathbf{q}_3, \ldots )`, then

.. math::

   Q^T Q &=
      \begin{pmatrix}
         \mathbf{q}_1 \\ \mathbf{q}_2 \\ \mathbf{q}_3 \\ \vdots
      \end{pmatrix}
      \begin{pmatrix}
         \mathbf{q}_1 & \mathbf{q}_2 & \mathbf{q}_3 & \cdots
      \end{pmatrix}

   &=
      \begin{pmatrix}
         \mathbf{q}_1^T \mathbf{q}_1
         & \mathbf{q}_1^T \mathbf{q}_2
         & \mathbf{q}_1^T \mathbf{q}_3
         & \cdots \\
         \mathbf{q}_2^T \mathbf{q}_1
         & \mathbf{q}_2^T \mathbf{q}_2
         & \mathbf{q}_2^T \mathbf{q}_3
         & \cdots \\
         \mathbf{q}_3^T \mathbf{q}_1
         & \mathbf{q}_3^T \mathbf{q}_2
         & \mathbf{q}_3^T \mathbf{q}_3
         & \cdots \\
         \vdots & & & \ddots
      \end{pmatrix}

Since :math:`Q^T Q = I`, it must be that :math:`\mathbf{q}_i^T \mathbf{q}_j =
\delta_ij`.  That is, *the column vectors of :math:`Q` form an orthogonal
basis.*

The natural orthogonality of :math:`Q` is the basis for the :math:`QR`
decomposition.
