=========================
Thomas Tridiagonal Solver
=========================

Solve a system of equations of the form

.. math::

   b_1 x_1 + c_1 x_2                   & = d_1 \\
   a_2 x_1 + b_2 x_2 + c_2 x_3         & = d_2 \\
   \vdots \\
   a_i x_{i-1} + b_i x_i + c_i x_{i+1} & = d_i \\
   \vdots \\
   a_n x_{n-1} + b_n x_n               & = d_n \\

:math:`x_1` can be expressed in terms of :math:`x_2`, which can be used in the
next expression to construct an expression for :math:`x_3`, and so on.
Successive expressions of :math:`x_i` into :math:`x_{i+1}` will eventually
terminate at :math:`x_n`, allowing for a backsubstitution through to
:math:`x_1`, solving the system.

The procedure become more clear after eliminating :math:`x_1`, so that

.. math::

   b_1 a_2 x_1 + c_1 a_2 x_2               & = d_1 a_2 \\
   b_1 a_2 x_1 + b_1 b_2 x_2 + b_1 c_2 x_3 & = b_1 d_2 \\
   \\
   \Rightarrow
      (b_1 b_2 - c_1 a_2) x_2 + (b_1 c_2) x_3 & = (b_1 d_2 - d_1 a_2) \\
   \Rightarrow
      \tilde{a}_2 x_1 + \tilde{b}_2 x_2 + \tilde{c}_2 x_3 & = \tilde{d}_2 \\

where

.. math::

   \tilde{a}_2 &= 0 \\
   \tilde{b}_2 &= b_1 b_2 - c_1 a_2 \\
   \tilde{c}_2 &= b_1 c_2 \\
   \tilde{d}_2 &= b_1 d_2 - d_1 a_2.

Now consider an induction argument where :math:`\tilde{a}_{i-1} = 0` and the
other coefficients :math:`\tilde{b}_{i-1}`, :math:`\tilde{c}_{i-1}`, and
:math:`\tilde{d}_{i-1}` are known.  Apply this method to the next expression
:math:`a_i x_{i-1} + b_i x_i + c_i x_{i+1} = d_i`.

.. math::

   \tilde{b}_{i-1} a_i x_{i-1} + \tilde{c}_{i-1} a_i x_i
      &= \tilde{d}_{i-1} a_i \\
   \tilde{b}_{i-1} a_i x_{i-1} + \tilde{b}_{i-1} b_i x_i
      + \tilde{b}_{i-1} c_i x_{i+1} &= \tilde{b}_{i-1} d_i \\
   \\
   \Rightarrow 
      (\tilde{b}_{i-1} b_i - \tilde{c}_{i-1} a_i) x_i
      + \tilde{b}_{i-1} c_i x_{i+1}
      & = (\tilde{b}_{i-1} d_i - \tilde{d}_{i-1} a_i)

From this, define the next iteration of coeffients.

.. math::

   \tilde{a}_i &= 0 \\
   \tilde{b}_i &= \tilde{b}_{i-1} b_i - \tilde{c}_{i-1} a_i \\
   \tilde{c}_i &= \tilde{b}_{i-1} c_i \\
   \tilde{d}_i &= \tilde{b}_{i-1} d_i - \tilde{d}_{i-1} a_i

This is the main result of the algorithm.  The transformed coordinates present
an upper triangular system of equations with a clear method of evaluation.

.. math::

   \tilde{b}_{i} x_i + \tilde{c}_i x_{i+1} &= \tilde{d}_i & i = 1, n-1 \\
   \tilde{b}_n x_n &= \tilde{d}_n

The algorithm is as follows:

1. Construct the coefficient arrays :math:`\tilde{b}_i`, :math:`\tilde{c}_i`,
   :math:`\tilde{d}_i` from 1 to n.

2. Then backtrack from n to 1 and evaluate :math:`x_i`.


Normalized form
---------------

The more common form of the expression is to normalize the results so that
:math:`\tilde{b} = 1`, so that

.. math::

   \tilde{a}_i &= 0 \\
   \tilde{b}_i &= 1 \\
   \tilde{c}_i &= \frac{c_i}{b_i - \tilde{c}_{i-1} a_i} \\
   \tilde{d}_i &= \frac{d_i - \tilde{d}_{i-1} a_i}{b_i - \tilde{c}_{i-1} a_i}

and

.. math::

   x_i + \tilde{c}_i x_{i+1} &= \tilde{d}_i & i = 1, n-1 \\
   x_n &= \tilde{d}_n

It greatly simplifies the expressions, but it does require additional divisions
in computing the coefficients.
