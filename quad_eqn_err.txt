Minimizing quadratic error
==========================

Solving the quadratic equation
------------------------------

Familiar to everyone, but let's get started.  I'll try to focus a bit more
carefully on the signs of the terms.

We seek solutions to the quadratic equation.

.. math::

   a x^2 + b x + c = 0

We are only interested in real coefficients (:math:`a,b,c \in R`) and, for the
most part, real solutions (:math:`x \in R`).

Assume that :math:`a \neq 0` and complete the square.

.. math::

   a \left( x^2 + \frac{b}{a} x + \frac{b^2}{4a} \right)
      - \frac{b^2}{4a} + c = 0

Simplify:
   
.. math::

   \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}

And then get the final pair of solutions.

.. math::

   x = -\frac{b}{2a} \pm \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2|a|},

Although the sign of :math:`a` has been lost to the square root of the RHS, it
can be integrated into the solution.  Write :math:`a` as :math:`\text{sgn}(a)
|a|`, where sgn() is the sign of a.

.. math::

   x = -\frac{b}{2a} \pm \text{sgn}(a) \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}

The pair of solutions is unchanged, regardless of the sign of :math:`a`.  So
without loss of generality,

.. math::

   x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}


Floating-point evaluation
-------------------------

Errors due to floating-point approximation can dominate calculations associated
with the discriminant, :math:`d = \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}`.

1. :math:`4ac \ll b^2`

   If :math:`4ac` is small, then the discriminant is approximately equal to
   :math:`b` when the signs differ.
